More Examples — Different Hint Types

Showing how tapping the blank works for nouns (case hints), separable verbs, and adjectives

Noun — Dative Case
Front (hint revealed)
Sentence Cloze · Context
Er erzählte dativ · neutraldem Kind eine Geschichte.
He told the child a story.
Learner knows "Kind" but isn't sure of article case. Tap the blank → hint says dativ · neutral → must be "dem Kind".
Separable Verb — Two Blanks
Front (hint revealed)
Sentence Cloze · Context
Sie präteritum · er/sie/es · trennbarzog ihre Jacke an.
She put on her jacket.
Two blanks for the separated prefix. Tap the first blank → hint präteritum · trennbar tells you tense + that the verb separates.
Adjective — Comparative
Front (hint revealed)
Sentence Cloze · Context

The weather is getting colder than yesterday.
Learner knows "kalt" but needs the comparative form. Tap the blank → hint komparativ → "kälter" (with umlaut).

Implementation Notes

Change required: Add hint_front=True to the cloze_picker_js() call in CLOZE_FRONT (update_templates.py). The blank span itself becomes the tap target — no extra elements added.

Key design choices in this mockup:

  • Zero visual footprint — the card looks identical to today. No icon, no indicator.
  • The cloze blank itself is the tap target. Tapping it toggles the hint tooltip above.
  • Hint is hidden by default — the card is exactly as hard as today if you don't tap.
  • On mobile, tapping the blank reveals the hint; tapping anywhere else dismisses it.
  • On desktop, clicking the blank toggles the hint (no hover — deliberate interaction only).
  • The tooltip uses the same pill shape, positioning, and caret arrow as the existing back-side tooltip.
  • If ClozeHint is empty for a variant, the blank behaves normally (no tap handler).

Self-assessment protocol:

  • Didn't need the hint → Good or Easy
  • Tapped the hint, then got it right → Hard
  • Tapped the hint and still got it wrong → Again

Why this works for learning: The hint provides retrieval support at the moment of maximum cognitive engagement (during the question). Research on "elaborative retrieval" shows that partial cues during retrieval practice improve long-term retention more than post-retrieval feedback alone — as long as the learner tries without the cue first. The tap-to-reveal interaction ensures the learner makes an initial attempt before scaffolding.
